## 1. What ISO/IEC 42001 certifies; what derivatives inherit

- Certification attaches to an ORGANIZATION'S AI management system
  (AIMS), never to a model artifact. ISO's own explainer: 42001 does not
  certify a model, dataset, or product.
  https://www.iso.org/home/insights-news/resources/iso-42001-explained-what-it-is.html
- IBM's "Granite is ISO 42001 certified" is ORGANIZATION-SCOPED: Granite
  4.0 announced as the first open-weight family "covered by an
  accredited ISO/IEC 42001:2023 certification" - what is certified is
  IBM's training-data governance/testing/documentation processes; the
  models are outputs of that certified system. Certifying body + exact
  AIMS scope: UNVERIFIED (IBM page blocked; secondary sources do not
  name the CB).
  https://digital.nemko.com/news/ibm-granite-40-first-iso-42001-certified-open-source-ai
- NO inheritance mechanism exists. Downstream, the upstream model is a
  supply-chain input under supplier due-diligence controls. Using a
  certified base eases due diligence; confers zero certification status.
  If Tessera wants the word "certified", Tessera's own AIMS - scoped to
  the post-training pipeline - must be audited.
- Tiny-org cost/timeline (2026): ~$10-40k all-in, 3-9 months; Stage 1+2
  audit fees ~$5-15k small-org; ANAB-accredited CBs: Schellman (first
  accredited), A-LIGN, BSI, DNV. ISO 27001 holders save 20-30%.
  https://elevateconsult.com/insights/iso-42001-certification-timeline-budget-for-founders/
- Lighter path: NIST AI RMF + Generative AI Profile (NIST-AI-600-1, Jul
  2024) - voluntary, publishable self-assessment, no audit.

## 2. Artifact-level trust chain standards

- CycloneDX ML-BOM: ML support since 1.5; 1.6 = ECMA-424; 1.7 (Oct
  2025) current. machine-learning-model + data component types +
  embedded model card. AI-BOMs moving into procurement expectations.
  https://cyclonedx.org/capabilities/mlbom/
- SPDX 3.0.1: AI + Dataset profiles (architecture, sources, licensing,
  trained-on/tested-on relationships).
  https://spdx.github.io/spdx-spec/v3.0.1/model/AI/AI/
- Model signing MATURE: OpenSSF Model Signing (OMS) v1.0 (Apr 2025);
  PKI-agnostic (Sigstore keyless w/ Rekor, X.509, PKCS#11); reference
  impl sigstore/model-transparency (`pip install model-signing`); Go
  port presented Feb 2026. HF native signature verification UI:
  UNVERIFIED.
  https://openssf.org/blog/2025/04/04/launch-of-model-signing-v1-0-openssf-ai-ml-working-group-secures-the-machine-learning-supply-chain/
- **How IBM signs Granite (the recipe to copy):** checkpoints ship
  `model.sig` produced by OMS against IBM's own Sigstore identity
  provider. Verification per IBM model cards:
  `python -m model_signing verify sigstore --signature model.sig
  --identity Granite.Preview@ibm.com
  --identity_provider https://sigstore.verify.ibm.com/oauth2 .`
  IBM also ships SIGNED LORA ADAPTERS (granitelib repos, e.g.
  granitelib-rag-r1.0, granitelib-guardian-r1.0, each with its own
  model.sig, identity Granite-sign@ibm.com) - direct precedent that
  adapter-level OMS signing is the established pattern.
  https://huggingface.co/ibm-granite/granite-4.0-tiny-preview
  https://huggingface.co/ibm-granite/granitelib-rag-r1.0
- C2PA spec 2.4 has explicit AI/ML guidance (manifests referencing
  model Content Credentials, training-input provenance); precedent
  mostly research (EKILA etc.); the ecosystem standard for distribution
  is OMS + BOMs. Natural design: C2PA-style per-sample ingredient
  manifests internally, bridged to Croissant/ML-BOM at export.
  https://spec.c2pa.org/specifications/specifications/2.4/ai-ml/ai_ml.html
- Croissant: JSON-LD dataset metadata (MLCommons), auto-generated by
  HF's viewer; Croissant 1.1 + RAI extension carries
  rai:dataCollection*, rai:personalSensitiveInformation, participant
  consent documentation, PROV-O/ODRL grounding.
  https://docs.mlcommons.org/croissant/docs/croissant-rai-spec.html

## 3. EU AI Act + GDPR for post-training

- GPAI "substantial modification" threshold CONFIRMED: downstream
  modifier becomes a GPAI model provider only when modification compute
  > 1/3 of original training compute (unknown original: 1/3 of 10^23
  FLOPs). LoRA (~10^16-10^19) is 4-7 orders below. Below threshold:
  AI SYSTEM provider only; document why below (per guidelines); even a
  crossing modifier owes obligations "limited to the modification".
  https://artificialintelligenceact.eu/gpai-guidelines-overview/
- Article 50 transparency applies from 2 Aug 2026 (unchanged by the
  Digital Omnibus, provisional agreement 7 May 2026, which deferred
  Annex III to 2 Dec 2027).
  https://www.gibsondunn.com/eu-ai-act-omnibus-agreement-postponed-high-risk-deadlines-and-other-key-changes/
- GDPR: EDPB Opinion 28/2024 - models trained on personal data are NOT
  automatically anonymous; case-by-case extraction/regurgitation
  assessment. For consumer-contributed productivity content the
  defensible basis is explicit granular CONSENT (Art 6(1)(a) + Art
  9(2)(a) for residual sensitive content), training named as a distinct
  purpose. Withdrawal is prospective; data must exit future runs.
  https://www.edpb.europa.eu/system/files/2024-12/edpb_opinion_202428_ai-models_en.pdf
- NO regulator mandates machine unlearning; the defensible 2026
  mechanism is dataset versioning + exclusion + periodic retrain
  (cheap for adapters), documented as the erasure-propagation
  procedure. Never promise "removed from the model".
  https://www.edps.europa.eu/data-protection/technology-monitoring/techsonar/machine-unlearning_en

## 4. Private HF dataset mechanics

- Plans: Free 100GB private; PRO ($9/mo) 1TB; Team ($20/user/mo) /
  Enterprise (from $50/user/mo) 1TB/seat, overage $18/TB/mo private.
  https://huggingface.co/docs/hub/storage-limits
- Access: private repos, fine-grained tokens, org roles; Resource
  Groups + SSO + audit logs (with export API) on Team/Enterprise;
  Storage Regions (EU) Enterprise-only - relevant for GDPR posture.
  Data Studio viewer on private datasets needs PRO+; the viewer is what
  auto-generates Croissant.
  https://huggingface.co/docs/hub/en/audit-logs
- Xet chunk-dedup backend default since May 2025 - incremental parquet
  appends and shard rewrites cheap (useful for revocation rewrites).
  https://huggingface.co/blog/migrating-the-hub-to-xet
- Scanning: pickle, ClamAV, TruffleHog secrets (verified secrets email
  the owner); flagged = marked, not blocked. **NO automatic PII
  scanning of dataset contents** - PII hygiene is entirely client-side.
  https://huggingface.co/docs/hub/security
- Upload API: upload_folder / upload_large_folder / CommitScheduler
  (periodic background commits) / create_commit; versioning via git
  refs; super_squash_history for hygiene. Parquet recommended.
  https://huggingface.co/docs/huggingface_hub/en/guides/upload
- Exposure: private repos "intended to be confidential"; no ToS clause
  granting HF training rights over repo content, but no affirmative
  no-training commitment at Free/PRO either; DPA/BAA/EU-region come
  with Enterprise; HF is SOC 2 Type 2; US jurisdiction; staff access
  per privacy policy. Self-hosted alternatives: lakeFS (acquired DVC
  Nov 2025) on own S3, at the cost of viewer/Croissant integration.

## 5. Consent + provenance

- ISO/IEC TS 27560:2023 "Consent record information structure" -
  machine-readable consent records/receipts (successor to Kantara
  Consent Receipt v1.1); W3C DPV guide maps 27560 to GDPR. Tessera's
  egress-grant receipts extended with 27560 fields become
  standards-conformant consent records almost for free.
  https://www.iso.org/standard/80392.html
  https://w3c-cg.github.io/dpv/guides/consent-27560
- Data Provenance Initiative: reference point for provenance norms;
  their position paper argues for per-sample cryptographically anchored
  provenance - exactly the receipt architecture.
  https://arxiv.org/pdf/2404.12691
- Anonymization: NER-scrub + rules + sampled human review is practice,
  but EDPB 28/2024 says scrubbing alone does not render data/models
  anonymous. DP-SGD over LoRA is practical at small scale (PEFT+DP is
  the standard pairing; Opacus supports it); real utility cost; a
  formal DP guarantee is the strongest evidence for the anonymity test.
- Revocation pattern: tombstone -> exclude from all future runs ->
  retrain/re-release affected adapters on a stated cadence -> record
  propagation in the receipt log.

## 7. The certifiability chain (per training run, in order)

1. Consent receipt bundle - ISO 27560 records (purpose "model
   post-training", HF named as processor, withdrawal method), one per
   contributing user, referenced per-sample.
2. Per-sample provenance manifest - content hash, source context,
   scrub-rule log + reviewer sign-off, consent-receipt ID, C2PA-style
   ingredient manifest anchored in the receipt log.
3. Dataset snapshot - immutable HF commit SHA + tag; Croissant 1.1 +
   RAI metadata.
4. Data BOM - SPDX 3.0 Dataset profile or CycloneDX data component.
5. Training-run record - base model ID + IBM signature verification
   transcript + hyperparameters + FLOPs estimate showing far below
   1/3 x 10^23 (the GPAI-guidelines "record why below threshold").
6. Eval gate report - pinned lm-eval/unitxt capability deltas vs base +
   refusal/jailbreak suite judged by Granite Guardian 4.1, thresholds
   declared BEFORE the run.
7. Adapter ML-BOM - CycloneDX 1.7 machine-learning-model component
   referencing 3-6.
8. Signed adapter - OMS model_signing, Sigstore keyless, stable Tessera
   org identity, Rekor entry (mirrors granitelib exactly).
9. Adapter model card - base_model relation, data summary, eval deltas,
   intended use/limitations.
10. Receipt-log anchor - hash of artifacts 1-9 appended to the signed
    receipt log; one tamper-evident head per run.
11. Release register + revocation ledger - dataset version per shipped
    adapter; withdrawal -> tombstone -> exclusion -> retrain queue ->
    superseded adapter revoked.
12. Org level (one-time): NIST AI RMF + GenAI Profile self-assessment
    FIRST; ISO 42001 AIMS (scoped to collection/curation/post-training/
    eval/release) as the later $15-40k capstone when revenue justifies.

## 8. HF pipeline recommendation

Private HF acceptable IF: (1) raw pre-scrub content never leaves the
device - only scrubbed, consented samples via an egress grant whose
consent names HF as processor + jurisdiction; (2) paid tier (PRO now;
Enterprise for EU region + DPA); (3) treat HF as
untrusted-for-confidentiality at the margin - client-side scrubbing is
the privacy boundary, not the "private" flag. Else lakeFS-on-S3 (EU).

Repo layout (`tessera/consented-interactions`, private):
```
data/contrib_date=YYYY-MM/user_bucket=NN/part-*.parquet
meta/croissant.jsonld
meta/consent_index.parquet     # sample_id -> consent_receipt_hash
meta/tombstones.parquet
meta/scrub_manifest.parquet    # per-sample rule log (no raw text)
README.md                      # dataset card + RAI summary
```
Consent receipts stay on-device; only hashes upload. user_bucket
partitioning makes one user's revocation rewrite only their shards
(cheap under Xet). Cadence: idle batching + CommitScheduler daily;
tag train-YYYYMMDD; training pins the commit SHA. Revocation: tombstone
-> physical shard rewrite next commit -> periodic history squash (note:
whether squash fully garbage-collects Xet chunks: UNVERIFIED - may need
HF support) -> exclusion -> retrain within a published SLA -> ledger.

## 9. Risks

1. MARKETING OVERREACH is the top legal risk. "Our models are ISO
   42001 certified like Granite" is false in every configuration. Safe
   claim: "built on ISO 42001-certified IBM Granite, post-trained under
   our documented management system, with signed artifacts and
   published evals."
2. Certification cannot be inherited or retrofitted onto past runs -
   the AIMS must exist before the audited period; artifacts 1-11 from
   day one ARE the audit evidence.
3. Free-text anonymization is never watertight - treat the HF dataset
   as personal data regardless of scrubbing; consent records, DPA,
   retention, erasure propagation are mandatory. DP-SGD is the only
   formal-claim path, at utility cost.
4. Unlearning is not a compliance mechanism in 2026 - promise
   exclusion-plus-retrain with a dated SLA.
5. Consent scope-creep: "heavier runs elsewhere" is a different
   processing operation - needs its own consent language + egress grant
   class UP FRONT; consent cannot be broadened retroactively.
6. Special-category leakage: scrub rules over-remove; Art 9(2)(a)
   language covers residuals.
7. Benign fine-tuning degrades safety (documented ~30% refusal drop) -
   no adapter ships without the Guardian-judged gate.
8. HF residual exposure at Free/PRO (no DPA, US jurisdiction, no PII
   scanning) - a scrub-pipeline bug uploads PII silently.
9. Cannot be made compliant, avoid: training on non-consented data
   "because it's local"; uploading raw pre-scrub text; claiming
   provenance for samples outside the grant flow; treating the GPAI
   threshold as an Article 50 exemption.
10. Sequencing: artifact chain first (mostly engineering adjacent to
    the receipt architecture, ~90% of buyer-visible trust); the 42001
    certificate is the later capstone. Certificate-first is backwards.
